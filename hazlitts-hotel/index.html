The House

The Last Home of William Hazlitt

Hazlitt's occupies a row of Georgian townhouses on Frith Street dating back to 1718. The essayist William Hazlitt — one of the masters of English prose — lived and died in the house in 1830; his blue plaque still hangs beside the front door.

Inside, nothing is standardised. Rooms are furnished with four-poster beds, antiques and old paintings — more than 2,000 paintings and prints line the walls — and the bathrooms keep their Victorian character, with claw-footed baths. Sloping floorboards, real fires and a famously quiet welcome make it a refuge from the pace of Soho outside.

It remains a favourite of writers and artists, who leave signed copies of their books behind — you'll find them in the Library.

Frequently Asked Questions

Anything else, our team answers personally — call, email or message us on WhatsApp, or use the guest chat on this page.

Where exactly is Hazlitt's?

6 Frith Street, Soho, London W1D 3JA — just off Soho Square. The nearest Underground stations are Tottenham Court Road and Leicester Square, both a few minutes' walk away.

What is included in every room?

Air-conditioning, free WiFi, a TV with Chromecast, complimentary spring water, a Nespresso machine with Allpress pods and land&water bath products — alongside the antique furnishings and original artwork each room is known for.

Is breakfast served at the hotel?

Yes — breakfast is freshly prepared and delivered to your room, and our room service menu is available 24 hours a day. There is also the Library honesty bar and the gin bar in The Wardrobe for drinks.

Can I hold a small private event?

Yes. The Wardrobe hosts private gatherings of up to six guests in the heart of Soho — half-day room hire from £300 and full-day hire from £450. Email reservations@hazlitts.co.uk for details.
